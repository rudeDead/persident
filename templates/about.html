🧬 Multi-Baseline Enhanced Integrated Gradients

Explainable AI for Drug Discovery & Molecular Property Prediction

🎯 Project Overview

This project introduces an explainability framework called Multi-Baseline Enhanced Integrated Gradients (MB-EIG) applied to Graphormer models for predicting three key molecular properties essential in drug discovery:

  • ESOL (Aqueous Solubility): Measures how well a molecule dissolves in water
  • Lipophilicity (logP): Quantifies the affinity of a molecule for lipid vs aqueous environments
  • FreeSolv (Hydration Free Energy): Thermodynamic favorability of transferring a molecule to water

🔬 MB-EIG Methodology: How It Works

Our approach uses dual baselines to provide chemically meaningful explanations

1 Clone the Molecule

We start with your input molecule and create a carbon-only clone (Inert Skeleton). This means replacing all heteroatoms (O, N, S, etc.) with carbon atoms while keeping the same structure. This gives us a "boring" version of the molecule to compare against.

2 Change Heteroatoms Gradually

We gradually transform the carbon-only skeleton back into the original molecule by slowly "turning on" the heteroatoms. At each step, we measure how the model's prediction changes. This is like watching a time-lapse of the molecule gaining its chemical personality.

3 Apply Integrated Gradients

Using calculus (Integrated Gradients), we track how sensitive the prediction is to each atom along this transformation path. Atoms that cause big changes in the prediction get high attribution scores. This tells us which atoms are most important for the property.

4 Combine with Zero Baseline

We also compute attributions from a "zero baseline" (empty molecule) and average it with the skeleton baseline. This dual-baseline approach makes our explanations more robust and reliable, capturing both relative (compared to skeleton) and absolute (compared to nothing) importance.

5 Fragment-Level Analysis

Finally, we group atoms into chemically meaningful fragments (like hydroxyl groups, aromatic rings, etc.) and aggregate their attributions. This gives you explanations in terms chemists actually think about, not just individual atoms.

📐 Mathematical Foundation

Standard Integrated Gradients

IGi(x) = (xi - x'i) × ∫α=01 ∂f(x' + α(x - x'))/∂xi dα

This computes the attribution for feature i by integrating gradients along the path from baseline x' to input x.

MB-EIG: Dual Baseline Enhancement

MB-EIGi = ½ × (IGiskeleton + IGizero)

We average attributions from two baselines: the carbon skeleton (chemically meaningful) and zero baseline (absolute importance).

Consistency Metric

Consistency = (Askeleton · Azero) / (||Askeleton|| × ||Azero||)

Cosine similarity between the two baseline attributions. High consistency (>0.7) indicates reliable explanations.

✅ Chemical Validation

How we ensure our explanations align with established chemistry

🔵 Polar Groups Increase Solubility

MB-EIG correctly identifies hydroxyl (-OH), carboxyl (-COOH), and amino (-NH₂) groups as increasing water solubility. These groups form hydrogen bonds with water molecules, making the molecule more "water-loving" (hydrophilic).

🔴 Hydrophobic Groups Decrease Solubility

Long alkyl chains and aromatic rings receive negative attributions for solubility, correctly reflecting their hydrophobic (water-hating) nature. These groups cannot form strong interactions with water.

⚖️ Lipophilicity Pattern Reversal

For lipophilicity (logP), the pattern reverses as expected: hydrophobic groups show positive contributions (favoring oil phase), while polar groups show negative contributions (favoring water phase). This validates that the model understands the underlying chemistry.

🧬 Fragment-Level Consistency

When we group atoms into functional groups, the aggregated attributions align with known fragment contribution methods (like Leo-Hansch for logP). This shows our atom-level explanations are chemically coherent.

📚 Research Background

This work addresses the critical gap in interpretability of deep learning models for molecular property prediction. Traditional methods like Integrated Gradients use unrealistic "zero baselines" that don't reflect true chemical structures. MB-EIG introduces chemically meaningful reference points using carbon and heteroatom baselines to capture realistic molecular contexts, enabling more transparent and trustworthy predictions.
