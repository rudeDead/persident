🧬 Explainable Molecular Property Prediction

🎯 What Makes MB-EIG Special?

Unlike traditional "black-box" AI, MB-EIG provides chemically meaningful explanations by comparing your molecule to its carbon-only skeleton. This dual-baseline approach reveals exactly which atoms and functional groups drive molecular properties.

✨ Core MB-EIG Features

🔬

Inert Skeleton Baseline

Creates a carbon-only clone of your molecule, then gradually "turns on" heteroatoms (O, N, S) to isolate their exact contribution to properties

📊

Integrated Gradients

Uses calculus to track how the prediction changes along the transformation path from skeleton to full molecule, providing precise atom attributions

🎯

Dual-Baseline Robustness

Averages explanations from skeleton and zero baselines for more reliable, consistent attributions (consistency scores >0.7)

🧪

Fragment-Level Analysis

Automatically groups atoms into functional groups (hydroxyl, aromatic, alkyl) and shows their collective impact on properties

🔄 How MB-EIG Works: 5 Simple Steps

1 Input Your Molecule

Enter a SMILES string (e.g., "CCO" for ethanol). Our system parses it into a molecular graph with atoms and bonds.

2 Create Skeleton Baseline

We replace all heteroatoms (O, N, S, etc.) with carbon to create an "inert skeleton" - your molecule's boring carbon-only twin.

3 Gradual Transformation

We gradually transform the skeleton back to your molecule, measuring how the prediction changes at each step using Integrated Gradients.

4 Compute Attributions

Each atom gets an attribution score showing its importance. We average scores from skeleton and zero baselines for robustness.

5 Fragment Analysis

Atoms are grouped into functional groups (hydroxyl, aromatic, etc.) with aggregated importance scores and chemical interpretations.

📈 Explanation Quality Metrics

Consistency Score

Measures agreement between skeleton and zero baseline attributions (cosine similarity)

✅ >0.7 = High
⚠️ 0.5-0.7 = Medium
❌ <0.5=Low

Confidence Score

Indicates how concentrated or distributed the importance is across atoms

High = Few atoms dominate
Low = Importance spread out

Validity Rating

Chemical plausibility based on known structure-property relationships

HIGH = Chemically sound
MEDIUM = Acceptable
LOW = Questionable
